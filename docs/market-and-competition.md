Status as of 31 October 2026. Figures are the best available; several are dated and marked so.

Working estimate: roughly 100,000 registered public-sector suppliers across the GCC, of which 10,000 to 15,000 bid often enough to need help but cannot justify a bid team. At AED 30,000 to 60,000 of outsourced bid spend per year each, the serviceable market is AED 300 to 900 million a year. A 1 to 2% success fee on Saudi SME awards alone would be a theoretical pool of SAR 220 to 440 million. Obtainable for a small firm within three years: AED 5 to 15 million a year in fees. The prime-bidding endgame is not capped by fees.

Etimad totals exclude large state-owned enterprises such as Aramco and PIF companies, which procure through their own systems. No GCC government publishes bidder counts; the Saudi figures come from one commercial analysis of Etimad award data.

Working estimate: about 25,000 Saudi public competitions a year at about 8.5 bidders each is roughly 200,000 bids, of which about a third, some 70,000, fail technical compliance before price is opened. Across the GCC, 110,000 to 150,000 procurement events a year, of which 35,000 to 45,000 are competitive tenders; this is extrapolated from Saudi Arabia, not measured. Annual UAE and Qatar counts need a portal request or a paid database such as MEED or Tendersinfo.

| UAE registration and document fees | portal registration free in Abu Dhabi, federal and RAK; document fees set per tender, as low as AED 50 | portals and notices; official |
| Bank charges for guarantees | not found | gap |
| Gulf bid costs measured directly | not found | gap |

Working estimate for a typical Saudi or UAE public competition: 40 to 120 hours over 2 to 4 weeks from 3 to 6 people, higher than Western RFPs because of Arabic documents, separate technical and financial envelopes, attested certificates and local-content forms. At a loaded AED 150 to 250 an hour that is AED 6,000 to 30,000 of internal effort per bid, consistent with 0.2% to 2% of the median Saudi public competition (about SAR 1.2 million). Bids of SAR 10 million and above run for months and cost 0.5% to 1% of value.

With a median of 6 bidders and a third of bids failing technical evaluation, a realistic win rate on open Saudi tenders is 15% to 25%. That puts the cost per contract won at 4 to 7 bids: roughly 200 to 800 hours and AED 30,000 to 150,000 on mid-size tenders. The case for the service is fewer hours per bid and fewer technical failures, not better prose.

## Competition

### Global RFP and proposal software

Loopio and Responsive sell enterprise seats on request. Arphie, Tribble, Inventive and AutogenAI are the AI-native generation; AutoRFP starts at about $899 a month. All are English-first, assume the client runs a proposal team, and know nothing of Etimad, Tejari, local content, ICV or Saudisation scoring. They are tools, not a done-for-you service.

### Gulf agencies

Labeeb in Dubai publishes prices from AED 2,500 per bid in the UAE and SAR 615 for a single proposal in Saudi, fixed fee, remote, no win guarantee, no success fee. Other providers are business-plan writers and PR firms that take tenders occasionally. No Gulf provider found offers a compliance matrix, a gap report, or measured outcomes.

### In-house bid managers

Present at larger suppliers, which marks the ceiling of the target segment.

### The client's own ChatGPT

Produces a generic narrative. Does not produce a clause-complete matrix, a gap report with deadlines, or a portal-ready pack, and invents claims. That is the line the service sells across.

### Big consultancies

Serve mega-bids. Not the segment.
