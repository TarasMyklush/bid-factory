# Bid library: onboarding schema

Everything we collect from a client in week one. Each item becomes one or more records with: id, type, title, date, owner, source file reference, validity or expiry, confidentiality tier, and a one-paragraph summary. Personal data is pseudonymised at indexing.

## 1. Company

- Legal entities, registrations and licences in each country, with numbers and expiry dates.
- Supplier registrations: Etimad, UAE federal Digital Procurement Platform, Dubai eSupply, Abu Dhabi, semi-government portals. Account holder role, renewal dates.
- Ownership structure and ultimate beneficial owners as registered. Local ownership percentages where scored.
- Saudi regional-headquarters licence status. UAE ICV certificate and score, Saudi local-content certificate and score, with dates.
- Headcount by country, nationality mix as needed for Saudisation and Emiratisation scoring. Numbers only, no names.
- Financials for the last three years as required by tenders: audited statements, turnover, bank reference letters. Stored as documents, summarised as figures.
- Insurance certificates: professional indemnity, public liability, cyber. Limits and expiry.
- Bank guarantee and bond capacity, with the issuing bank.

## 2. Certifications and accreditations

- ISO 9001, 27001, 20000, 22301, 14001, 45001 and others, with certificate number, scope, body, expiry.
- CMMI level, SOC reports, PCI DSS, cloud provider partner tiers, vendor certifications (Microsoft, Oracle, SAP, Cisco, and so on) at company and individual level.
- Sector-specific: NCA ECC compliance statements, SAMA or CBUAE attestations where held, Dubai Electronic Security Center, health or education accreditations.
- Memberships and awards.

## 3. Past performance

One record per project, each with: client, sector, country, dates, contract value band, scope, our role (prime or sub), technologies, team size, outcomes with numbers, reference letter on file (yes or no), reference contact available (organisation and role only until bid confirmed), and permission to cite (public, on request, no).

Target: twenty records minimum, ten with reference letters.

## 4. Products, services and partners

- Service catalogue with a one-page description each, standard SLAs, delivery methodology, and tooling.
- Products: versions, hosting options, certifications, data residency options, licensing models.
- Partners and OEMs: agreements, authorisation letters, support levels, local presence.
- Subcontractors used in past bids and their capability statements.

## 5. People

- Role profiles for every role that appears in bids: title, years of experience band, certifications, languages, nationality where scored, availability. Names and contact details are removed at indexing and restored by the client at submission.
- Organisation chart by function.
- Key personnel CVs as source documents in the client's tenant, referenced by id.

## 6. Policies and boilerplate

- Quality, information security, business continuity, health and safety, environmental, anti-bribery, data protection, modern slavery, code of conduct, Saudisation and Emiratisation plans. Each as a document plus a two-paragraph summary.
- Standard sections from past bids: company overview, methodology, project management approach, transition and exit, risk management, knowledge transfer, warranty and support.
- Standard forms already completed: vendor registration, conflict of interest, integrity pledge, bank details, authorised signatory.

## 7. Commercial

- Rate cards by role and country. Standard discount policies. Payment terms accepted. Currency and VAT treatment.
- Pricing sheet template the client will use for each bid. Our drafting uses only numbers from this sheet.

## 8. Bid history

- Every bid in the last 24 months: tender, buyer, date, outcome, score and feedback where released, reasons for loss as understood. This is the baseline the shortlist-rate metric is measured against.

## 9. Preferences and constraints

- Buyers to avoid. Minimum contract value. Regions. Appetite for consortiums. Signatories and approval chain. Turnaround expectations. House style and templates.

## 10. Data handling

- Which tier the client's own library material falls under.
- Tenant access granted (SharePoint or Google Drive location, scoped permissions, named users).
- The signed data handling sheet.
